Dear Editors of *Ecological Informatics*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Record-entry selection changes ecological estimands and limits the transport of detection correction,”** for publication in *Ecological Informatics*.

Automated ecological monitoring is increasingly built as a pipeline from sensors to event records, classification and ecological analysis. Most downstream workflows, however, begin only after a sensor or algorithm has created an event row. Our study asks what ecological information is lost during that earlier transformation and what provenance must be retained to diagnose and, where possible, correct it.

We develop an auditable record-entry measurement contract linking an exposure/reference denominator, pre-entry trigger or gate provenance, usable record entry, downstream semantic processing and a frozen ecological estimand. We evaluate the same contract in two contrasting observation modalities: CCTV-referenced camera traps and continuous expert-annotated acoustic recordings.

The empirical results show that the issue is not simply reduced sample size. In 881 CCTV-confirmed fox/badger passes, species composition changed substantially between the reference, triggered and confirmed-capture worlds, and the shift persisted after standardizing to a common physical camera-position distribution. In protected continuous-acoustic data, oracle-perfect downstream semantics could not reconstruct a temporal ecological contrast after true windows had already been removed upstream. Entry-aware correction reduced composition error in several held-out contexts and in two ecological endpoints, but a frozen simultaneous camera-plus-position transport test increased error. Thus the entry process and the correction derived from it both have observation-context domains.

We believe the manuscript fits *Ecological Informatics* because it addresses ecological data acquisition and transformation in automated sensor systems, uncertainty that arises before conventional event-table analysis, reproducible data provenance, and the transferability of correction across observation contexts. The contribution is accompanied by executable validation code, pinned external-data workflows, machine-readable results and scripts that regenerate all manuscript figures from committed evidence.

We explicitly do not claim discovery of imperfect detection or camera-trap false negatives. The contribution is an estimand-centred audit of how automated record construction changes the ecological information supplied to later analysis, together with practical data-management requirements for making that selection diagnosable and its correction testable.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
