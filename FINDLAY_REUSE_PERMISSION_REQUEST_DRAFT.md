**Subject:** Permission to reuse CT-Detection data in a methodological reanalysis

Dear Dr Findlay,

I am preparing a methodological paper on how camera-trap record-entry processes can change ecological quantities available to downstream analysis. The study reanalyses the public data in your GitHub repository `melaniefindlay/CT-Detection`, associated with Findlay, Briers & White (2020), *Component processes of detection probability in camera-trap studies: understanding the occurrence of false-negatives*.

Specifically, our reproducible analyses use the public files:

- `REGISTRATION_FOX_BADGER.csv`;
- `TRIGGER_OTTER_WET.DRY.csv`;

from repository commit `abc72f535bb59ebed202fb7acca852fc1647e97a`.

We cite your original paper prominently and explicitly state that the camera-trap false-negative patterns, CCTV reference design, and sequential detection-process decomposition are findings/prior contributions of your study rather than discoveries of our reanalysis. Our additional analyses focus on how reference-pass composition changes after trigger/capture, position-standardized composition contrasts, and retrospective correction/transport tests.

The GitHub repository is publicly accessible but does not currently display an explicit repository licence. For journal submission, we would therefore like to confirm permission rather than infer reuse rights from public availability.

Would you be willing to confirm in writing that we may:

1. analyse these two public CSV datasets for this methodological paper;
2. report derived numerical summaries and figures based on them; and
3. provide reproducible code that downloads the source files from your original public repository without redistributing the original CSV files ourselves?

If there is a preferred licence, data citation, acknowledgement, or additional condition you would like us to use, please let us know and we will follow it.

Thank you for making the original reference-camera data publicly accessible and for considering this request.
